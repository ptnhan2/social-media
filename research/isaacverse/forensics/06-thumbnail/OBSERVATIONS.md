# Video 06 — Thumbnail tutorial visual evidence

Source: `06 - How I Actually Make Viral Thumbnails.mp4` (1280x720, 60fps).
Contact sheets sampled at 15-second intervals.

## Evidence-backed construction

- The tutorial itself uses a **black presentation stage** with neon cyan/yellow/green/red connector lines and glowing rounded boxes.
- Concepts such as rule-of-thirds, palette and thumbnail types are rendered as animated diagrams, not spoken-only instruction.
- YouTube homepage/search screenshots are repeatedly staged as evidence. Thumbnails are shown at realistic browse scale, then enlarged for inspection.
- Isaac's recurring orange-haired character is composited into thumbnail examples and UI scenes; he functions as a host and visual pointer, not just a logo.
- Large yellow chapter typography (`THUMBNAILS`) is used as a visual anchor over a moving/blurred thumbnail wall.
- Radial blur/zoom and glow are used to transition into the thumbnail workspace and to direct attention to a chosen example.
- The tutorial alternates between abstract diagram explanation, real-world homepage evidence, and Photoshop/Canva construction. The sequence follows teaching logic, not a fixed layout.
- The same semantic object can appear as: diagram node, YouTube thumbnail, Photoshop canvas, blurred background, and final composition. This is the same transformation pattern observed in video 04 comments and video 03 voice props.

## Implication

Thumbnail design is not an isolated asset generator. It shares the IsaacVerse edit grammar: explain a concept with a diagram, ground it in real evidence, bring the host into the frame, then show construction and result. The future agent needs a semantic **explanation shot sequence**, not only a thumbnail template.
