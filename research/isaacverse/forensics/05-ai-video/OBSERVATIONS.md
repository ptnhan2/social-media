# Video 05 — AI video attempt visual evidence

Source: `05 - I Tried Making a Viral AI Video.mp4` (1280x720, 60fps).
Contact sheets sampled at 20-second intervals.

## Evidence-backed construction

- The episode alternates between the AI-production process and the story of trying to finish the project.
- Tool interfaces (thumbnail generator, Midjourney, Runway, PikaLabs, prompt panels, image grids, timelines) are staged with Isaac as a host/guide. They are not raw tutorials pasted in sequence.
- Storyboard and prompt design are visualized with black-stage diagrams: nodes, curved connector lines, labeled branches, color-coded categories and large semantic words such as `Images`, `Videos`, `parameters`, `art styles`, `camera angles`, `characters`.
- Style consistency and character consistency are made visible as a problem: reference images, character sheets, prompt variants, side-by-side outputs and failed attempts appear as evidence.
- Generated cinematic frames are interleaved with Isaac/Steven scenes in a room, preserving the narrative conflict instead of separating “tutorial” and “story” into different sections.
- The edit shows failures and uncertainty visually (bad outputs, inconsistent characters, tool comparisons, progress/days-left cards). This supports the anti-soulless/learning-in-public identity.
- Timeline frames show multiple colored tracks for clips, music and sound effects; the edit process itself becomes a visual object.
- The recurring black + neon diagram grammar is used for abstract process explanation, while generated shots use cinematic color and framing. The system switches visual grammar based on semantic role.

## Implication

For AI-agent migration, the unit cannot be "image + zoom". A production beat may require a **process choreography**: show tool/context → expose decision/problem → display candidate outputs → compare/fail → move into narrative scene → return to production timeline. The agent needs semantic beat types and transitions between visual worlds.
