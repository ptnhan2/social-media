# Why AI Dialogue Sounds Like Therapy — Story Doc

## Audience
- Persona: fiction writer using AI tools (Sudowrite, Claude, ChatGPT)
- Level: intermediate — has tried AI writing, noticed the "therapy speak" problem
- Platform: YouTube (Craft × AI channel)

## Common Goal
- Viewer: write AI-assisted fiction with authentic, distinct character voices
- Creator: establish the channel as the go-to source for AI × craft analysis

## Surface Problem
AI-generated dialogue always defaults to therapy-speak: "I hear you saying
that...", "That must be really hard...", "It sounds like you're feeling..."
— every character becomes a counselor.

## Deeper Problem
The fear that AI will homogenize all fiction into one empathetic voice —
that no amount of prompting will produce characters who interrupt, lie, or
simply don't care. Writers feel the tool is fighting their creative intent.

## Transformation
- Start: "AI dialogue is broken and I can't fix it"
- End: "I can name the 3 therapy patterns and break them deliberately"

## Thumbnail Promise
You'll see the exact before/after of AI dialogue — same scene, one version
sounds like a support group, the other sounds like fiction.

## Hero Journey (condensed for 30s)

| Slot | Beat | Function |
|---|---|---|
| status_quo | Hook: "Why does every AI character sound like a therapist?" | name the false solution (AI as-is) |
| call | The 3 patterns: validation, reflection, emotional labeling | reframe the unit of editing |
| trials | Evidence: community consensus ("dialogue is AI fiction's weakest link") | show evidence in context |
| approach | WHY: RLHF rewards empathy → therapy speak emerges | give a repeatable process |
| crisis | Before/after comparison: therapy version vs fiction version | compare the meaningful choice |
| reward | The fix: give permission to interrupt, disagree, lie | deliver the usable rule |
| return | AI can write sharp dialogue when you break the patterns | resolve with a memorable rule |

## Script (voiceover, ~75 words / 30 seconds)

Ever noticed AI characters always sound like they're in a therapy session?
"I hear you saying that." "That must be really hard." This isn't a bug —
it's RLHF's fingerprint. Models get rewarded for empathy, so every
character becomes a counselor. The fix: break the three patterns. Stop
validating. Stop reflecting. Stop labeling emotions. Give your characters
permission to interrupt, disagree, and lie. That's when AI dialogue starts
sounding like fiction instead of a support group.
