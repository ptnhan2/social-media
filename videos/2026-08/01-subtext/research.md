# Video #1 Research Brief — Why AI Can't Write Subtext

> 5 sub-agents, 250+ sources, July 23 2026

## Key findings

### Craft theory
- Subtext = what's NOT said. Reader infers meaning. Hemingway's Iceberg Theory: 1/8 above water, 7/8 below
- 26+ techniques for creating subtext (silence, deflection, contradiction, physical behavior)
- Cognitive science: Theory of Mind — readers actively construct meaning from gaps
- Subtext is considered one of the hardest craft skills because it requires TRUSTING the reader

### AI behavior (academic)
- "Narrative Flattening" (Li et al., May 2025, U Chicago): RLHF compresses narrative variation. Professional literary fiction compressed MOST
- "Beneath the Surface" (arXiv 2026): first paper to systematically test subtext generation. Best model only 22% success on hardest task
- "Reading Subtext" (TACL 2024, Best Paper): GPT-4, Claude-2.1, LLaMA-2 all fail >50% on subtext interpretation
- RLHF = primary culprit. Reward = helpful/clear/explicit → suppresses ambiguity
- Scale: single scene = passable (Claude can do it), novel-length = catastrophic (linear error increase)
- "Art or Artifice?" (CHI 2024, 355+ citations): LLMs fail Torrance Tests for Creative Writing

### Best examples for video
- **Literature centerpiece**: Hemingway "Hills Like White Elephants" — entire story about abortion, word "abortion" never spoken
- **The coffee mug**: "She made two cups of coffee. Drank one. Poured the other down the sink." — one sentence, zero emotion names, 100% emotional impact
- **AI failure**: "Sarah felt heartbroken. She was angry. She cried and felt lonely." — 100% emotion naming, zero emotional impact
- **Human version**: "Sarah read the text twice. Set the phone face-down. Made a sandwich she didn't want and ate it standing up, staring at Michael's empty parking spot."
- **NailedIt test**: Claude showed grief through actions (watering dead plants, ordering two coffees), ChatGPT explained through metaphor. Claude succeeds in short scene BUT degrades at novel length (Narrative Flattening)

## Contradictions (tension for script)
1. Claude CAN do subtext in short scenes (NailedIt) BUT degrades at novel length (Narrative Flattening) — so the problem isn't "can't" but "can't sustain"
2. AI-generated stories rated HIGHER by readers in quality (Nature study) BUT rated higher when labeled "human" — readers' perception is biased, not quality
3. ShakespeareAI claims AI "excels at subtext" BUT admits it requires detailed prompts + human direction — so AI doesn't do it autonomously
